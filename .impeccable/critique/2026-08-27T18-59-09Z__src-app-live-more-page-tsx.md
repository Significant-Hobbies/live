## Design Specificity Verdict

The exact-dream promise, warm paper and field palette, non-shaming language, and private Life Atlas metaphor are authored for Live. The initial equal territory grid and appended daily/history/game sections made the chassis more interchangeable than the language. Assessment B's source detector returned zero findings. Browser overlay signals were either outside this target or false positives: the plain hero eyebrow was mislabeled as a chip; Geist/Fraunces are the intentional two-font system; a layout transition came from the browser environment; and a nested-card signal belonged to the removed daily-history empty state.

## Overall Impression

The opening is expansive, adult, and emotionally safe. The biggest opportunity is to make the atlas structural and end on one credible next move instead of turning wonder into catalogue work.

## What's Working

- Exact wording, duplicate prevention, and failure preservation protect the user's intent.
- The hero's type, color, proportion, and privacy message make possibility feel personal rather than productive.
- “Dreams are not a contract” releases guilt without introducing scores, streaks, or shame.

## Priority Issues

- **P1 — Focus the operating model:** Search, import, discovery, atlas management, daily novelty, history, and games competed on one route. Keep the page about discovering and preserving dreams, then finding a first door.
- **P1 — Make relevance confidence-only:** Loose related results can undermine the promise that Live is honest about catalogue boundaries. Show only developed native matches; otherwise preserve the exact dream and open external research.
- **P1 — Make the atlas structural:** Replace the equal six-card catalogue with a branching route and progressive territory reveal.
- **P2 — Clarify action grammar:** Use Save/Keep for preservation, See the path for developed detail, and Find a first door for the next small move.
- **P2 — Tighten accessibility:** Give repeated door links contextual accessible names and keep all discovery/import targets at least 44px.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Alex:** Bulk paste is efficient, but broad card-level results slow expert triage. Confidence-only results and progressive territory reveal reduce that work.
- **Sam:** Repeated “Open a door” labels were indistinguishable in a link list; contextual names and 44px targets are required.
- **Casey:** On mobile, the saved atlas and real next move sat below thousands of pixels of catalogue and adjacent-product content. The route needs a shorter, progressive path.

## Minor Observations

- The privacy status above the hero is useful but visually detached.
- “419 native possibilities” proves breadth but can sound inventory-led.
- The intentional two-font system and dashed empty-state signal were detector false positives, not target defects.

## Questions to Consider

- If this page could accomplish only one job after someone has six dreams, what should it be?
- If the atlas words disappeared, would the structure still feel like wayfinding?
- Is an honest “we do not know yet” more valuable than a weak related result?
