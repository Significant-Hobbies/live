## Design Specificity Verdict

The result is authored for Significant Hobbies. The daylight hero, candid lake photography, direct life-first language, privacy reassurance, serif voice, and context-aware path from a large possibility to a small quest express the Life Atlas rather than a generic planner. The discovery area still uses cards, but the featured-first hierarchy and progressive three-at-a-time presentation avoid a flat catalog.

## Overall Impression

Live More now begins with one emotionally clear invitation, reveals breadth without dumping the corpus onto the user, and carries a chosen ambition into a genuinely related, smaller action. The largest remaining opportunity is to move even further from cards toward connected atlas/path composition in a later preserve-mode pass.

## What’s Working

- The hero makes leaving the screen feel more attractive than admiring a planning dashboard.
- Discovery does heavy internal work across more than 300 possibilities while presenting only three decisions at once.
- Local privacy, save feedback, undo, accessible labels, and a semantic small-step handoff make the experience trustworthy.

## Priority Issues

- **[P2] Ancillary touch sizing:** the third-party Feedback control remains below the project’s preferred 44px target. The core product controls meet it.
- **[P2] Time-horizon mixing:** large someday ambitions can still lead a section introduced by near-term hero language. A later pass could visibly distinguish “this week” from “someday.”
- **[P3] Token debt:** route components repeat approved palette hex values instead of using semantic design tokens.
- **[P3] Mobile ending:** the sitemap footer remains long relative to the action surface, although its links are now grouped and touch-friendly.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan, first-timer:** the first action and save/small-step choices are clear. “More than 300 possibilities” communicates breadth without the former internal “corpus” wording.
- **Sam, accessibility-dependent:** keyboard order, semantic landmarks, Axe baseline, focus, live feedback, and 44px primary controls pass. The external Feedback widget remains the one undersized ancillary control.
- **Casey, distracted mobile user:** local draft persistence, three-at-a-time discovery, large controls, and contextual continuation survive interruption better than the earlier six-card field.

## Questions to Consider

- Should a future discovery pass visibly split “do this week” from “keep for someday”?
- Can the next preserve-mode iteration express the three choices as directions on a path rather than cards without sacrificing scanning?
